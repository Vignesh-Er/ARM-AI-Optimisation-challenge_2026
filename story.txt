PACI (Physics-Informed Anomaly Classification for TinyML) tackles the challenge of monitoring semiconductor plasma etch processes efficiently on resource-constrained embedded systems. Traditional Always-On CNN models suffer from severe energy drain and frequent false alarms caused by natural equipment drift. PACI solves this using an ultra-low-power, 3-tier adaptive cascade architecture.  Tier 0 leverages a lightweight scalar Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) combined with statistical Normalized Innovation Squared (NIS) gating. This physics-informed layer tracks physical dynamics at just 23.98 us per step, dynamically adapting to benign drift while filtering out 84% of normal operations. Only significant anomalies escalate to neural layers.  Tier 1 employs a heavily quantized 4-bit (INT4) CMSIS-NN 1D-CNN, resolving most easy anomalies.

PACI (Physics-Informed Anomaly Classification for TinyML) tackles the challenge of monitoring semiconductor plasma etch processes efficiently on resource-constrained embedded systems. Traditional Always-On CNN models suffer from severe energy drain and frequent false alarms caused by natural equipment drift. PACI solves this using an ultra-low-power, 3-tier adaptive cascade architecture. Tier 0 leverages a lightweight scalar Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) combined with statistical Normalized Innovation Squared (NIS) gating. This physics-informed layer tracks physical dynamics at just ~465 ns per step, dynamically adapting to benign drift while filtering out non-anomalous operations. Only significant anomalies escalate to neural layers. Tier 1 employs a heavily quantized 4-bit (INT4) CMSIS-NN 1D-CNN, resolving most easy anomalies. Finally, ambiguous cases trigger the full Tier 2 8-bit (INT8) classifier. PACI is a physics-informed 3-tier cascade for Arm Cortex-M-class edge AI that avoids unnecessary neural inference. On a deterministic 2,000-step synthetic process trace, PACI reduces end-to-end compute by 90.58% and achieves a 10.62× effective speedup versus always-on INT8 inference, while maintaining reference-model classification parity on the validated test set.
